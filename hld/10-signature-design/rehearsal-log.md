# Rehearsal log

The design is not finished when the document is written. It is finished when you
have presented it out loud, timed, three times. Reading it back to yourself does
not count and neither does thinking it through on a walk — the failures this
catches are pacing failures, and pacing only breaks when you are actually
talking.

## How to run one

Set a forty-five minute timer. Stand up, use a whiteboard or a sheet of paper,
and present as though someone is in the room. No notes and no document open. Go
in the order of the framework: requirements, estimation, API and decision
record, the two paths, the deep dives, the trade-off, failure modes.

Record the third run. You will not enjoy it. Pacing problems are obvious on
playback and completely invisible from the inside, which is the whole reason to
do it, and the things that make you wince are a shorter list than you fear.

When the timer stops, stop, even mid-sentence. Then fill in a row below before
you do anything else, while you can still remember where it went slack.

## What to watch for

The failure here is not running dry. It is loving your own system too much:
spending eleven minutes on the feature store because you know it well, and
arriving at minute thirty with the trade-off and the failure modes still unsaid.
The parts you enjoy explaining are the parts to time most strictly.

Two checkpoints. At minute ten you should be finished with estimation. At minute
twenty-five you should be in the deep dives. If either slips by more than three
minutes, that is what the fix column is for.

## After the third

Two things. Write down the three sentences that came out best and keep them
word for word, because those are the ones worth saying identically every time.
Then take whatever you fumbled and check whether the fault is in the delivery or
in the document — if you keep stumbling in the same place, the document is
wrong there, and the fix belongs in
[design-doc-template.md](design-doc-template.md) rather than in more practice.
